Abstract—
Deploying monolithic 70-billion-parameter language models on consumer laptops and edge hardware (<8 GB RAM) triggers immediate out-of-memory (OOM) failures, while conventional multi-agent debate floods compact context windows with quadratic conversational transcripts, squandering up to 60% of inference compute on redundant pleasantries. We introduce CoDraft+, a resource-aware collaborative framework in which heterogeneous small language models (12M–39M parameters) iteratively build, refine, and verify one shared answer inside a Shared Canonical Workspace (Dt). Instead of exchanging free-form dialogue, specialist agents emit structured JSON patches reconciled through a 4-tier ResilientPatcher (strict JSON, fenced-block extraction, curly-brace newline sanitization, and heuristic prose fallback), tracked by a claim-centric EvidenceLedger (Lt) and governed by a multi-signal AdaptiveController combining query complexity C(q), epistemic uncertainty Ut, edit churn Δdiff,t, and verification pressure Vt. Evaluated strictly on genuine FP32 CPU forward passes across three independent architectural families—Set 1 (Pythia / GPT-NeoX), Set 2 (BabyLM / GPT-BERT), and Set 3 (Stentor2 / Llama)—CoDraft+ achieves an empirical breakthrough in Set 1: pairing Pythia-14M and Pythia-31M (44.56M active parameters) reaches 8.25% ROUGE-L, outperforming the 1.58× larger monolithic Pythia-70M baseline (4.59%) by +79.7% and an isolated parallel ensemble (5.71%) by +44.5%, while consuming less peak resident memory (1234.1 MB vs. 1250.1 MB). With equal scientific rigor, we document two critical negative results: context-horizon collapse in un-tuned 512-token BabyLM models and asymmetric capability dilution when Stentor2-12M edits a stronger Stentor2-30M draft. All 10 validation gates (G1–G10), 12/12 unit tests, and raw benchmark logs are openly reproducible with zero synthetic data.

I. Introduction

Consider an edge computing deployment: two commodity laptops or industrial edge micro-servers operating in an offline research station or rural clinic must synthesize an accurate technical brief from heterogeneous local observations. Neither machine possesses datacenter GPUs; each operates strictly on CPU threads with less than 8 GB of resident RAM. Loading a monolithic 70-billion-parameter large language model (LLM) causes immediate operating-system out-of-memory (OOM) termination. Conversely, relying on a single sub-50-million-parameter small language model (SLM) produces shallow, repetitive, or factually incomplete drafts.

To bridge this capability gap without exceeding edge memory envelopes, recent studies have explored multi-agent systems (MAS) in which multiple language models collaborate to solve complex tasks. However, the dominant multi-agent paradigm—conversational debate [2, 3]—treats models as chat participants appending full-length responses to a shared message history. Under edge constraints, conversational debate suffers from two structural bottlenecks: (1) quadratic token growth O(T2) across T rounds, where up to 60% of context capacity is consumed by meta-conversational pleasantries, restatements, and turn-taking boilerplate; and (2) fragile structured output parsing, where sub-50M models routinely break strict JSON schemas, causing naive multi-agent pipelines to crash or silently drop edits.

Human software engineers and technical authors collaborate differently under bounded cognitive bandwidth. Rather than reciting entire documents aloud in alternating turns, they work asynchronously on a shared canonical artifact—such as a version-controlled repository or collaborative manuscript. Contributors inspect the current canonical state, propose localized patches targeting specific sections, record verifiable factual claims, and halt once incremental edits stabilize.

We operationalize this canonical editing workflow in CoDraft+, an open-source, resource-aware multi-SLM collaboration engine engineered specifically for consumer hardware (<8 GB RAM). CoDraft+ replaces dialogue transcripts with a versioned, section-addressed workspace Dt persisted in SQLite and append-only JSONL logs. A deterministic QueryAnalyzer routes simple queries directly to a single-round fast path, while complex tasks invoke alternating specialist SLMs coordinated by a multi-signal AdaptiveController, a 4-tier ResilientPatcher, and a claim-tracking EvidenceLedger.

This paper makes five concrete contributions to edge AI and software engineering for multi-agent systems:

  • Canonical Blackboard Architecture for Edge SLMs: We formalize and implement a complete 14-module Python architecture (1,548 LOC) decoupling communication bandwidth from turn count T, eliminating conversational bloat while keeping workspace storage under 15 KB.
  • Superadditive Sub-50M Collaboration: On Set 1 (Pythia / GPT-NeoX), CoDraft+ pairing Pythia-14M + 31M (44.56M active) achieves 8.25% ROUGE-L, surpassing the 1.58× monolithic Pythia-70M baseline (4.59%) by +79.7% and the isolated ensemble control (5.71%) by +44.5%, at lower peak RAM (1234.1 MB vs. 1250.1 MB).
  • 4-Tier Fault-Tolerant JSON Patching & Multi-Signal Stopping: We design a 4-tier parsing cascade (codraft/patcher.py) achieving 0% unrecoverable patch drops across non-instruction-tuned SLMs, coupled with a calibrated 4-signal stopping controller (wc=0.35, wu=0.25, wd=0.25, wv=0.15).
  • Transparent Negative Results & Boundary Laws: We empirically identify and diagnose two exact failure boundaries: 512-token context-window collapse in BabyLM (Set 2) and asymmetric capability dilution in Stentor2 (Set 3).
  • Strict Zero-Synthetic-Data Verification: Every latency, RAM, perplexity, and accuracy metric reflects authentic FP32 CPU execution on 8 frozen Hugging Face checkpoints, audited by 10 validation gates (G1–G10) and 12/12 unit tests.

A. Iterative Self-Editing vs. Multi-Agent Debate

PEER [1] demonstrated that decomposing generation into iterative planning, editing, and explanation steps substantially improves factual precision and readability, but relies on a single fine-tuned monolithic model. Multiagent Debate [2] and Divergent Debate [3] showed that pitting multiple LLMs against one another reduces hallucinations on reasoning benchmarks. Yet dialogue-based debate serializes complete prior utterances at every turn, causing context length to grow quadratically with round count T and agent count K. On sub-50M edge models restricted to 512–2048 context tokens, dialogue transcripts rapidly saturate positional encodings.

B. Blackboard Architectures & Shared State Coordination

CoDraft+ adapts classic blackboard control architectures [4] to modern neural language models. In a blackboard system, heterogeneous knowledge sources never address each other directly; instead, they read a compact, canonical workspace and post structured updates mediated by a central controller. By exchanging localized section patches rather than full transcripts, CoDraft+ keeps input prompt size bounded by the length of the evolving answer |Dt| rather than the cumulative history ∑i=1..t |Ri|.

C. Cost-Aware Cascading & Edge Language Models

Model cascading frameworks such as FrugalGPT [15] reduce API expenditure by invoking inexpensive models first and escalating to larger cloud models only when confidence fails, while speculative decoding [16] accelerates a target large model using a draft model residing concurrently in VRAM. Quantized adapters such as QLoRA [12] compress memory footprints for single models. CoDraft+ addresses a distinct regime: strictly local CPU execution where no large cloud fallback or multi-model VRAM residency is available, achieving quality exceeding a larger single model through interleaved collaboration between two small specialists.

III. Methodology & Mathematical Formalism

Every mathematical formulation in this section maps directly to the executable Python implementation in codraft/.

A. Query Complexity Analysis & Fast-Path Routing

Before instantiating multi-turn collaboration, QueryAnalyzer (codraft/analyzer.py) classifies incoming query q into one of five domains—code (wdom=0.50), reasoning (0.45), creative (0.25), general (0.20), or factual (0.15)—using lexical keyword overlap and syntactic pattern matching (code fences, SQL/Python keywords, mathematical operators). It computes a deterministic complexity score C(q) ∈ [0.05, 1.0]:

C(q) = clip[0.05, 1](0.25·min(1, |q|/40) + min(1, 0.15N?) + wdom + min(0.15, 0.03Nsym)) (1)

where |q| is the token count, N? is the number of question marks, and Nsym counts structural/mathematical symbols. If C(q) < θfast = 0.40 and rounds are not forced, AdaptiveController activates a single-model fast path (T=1), avoiding unnecessary multi-turn latency on simple factual lookups.

B. Shared Canonical Workspace & Section Addressing

The canonical workspace state Dt = (full_textt, St, vt) maintains both the assembled document string and an ordered dictionary of Markdown sections St = {s1, …, sm} parsed via heading markers (#). When a specialist model Mk ∈ {MA, MB} emits a mutation PatchOperation Πt = (sec_id, op, content, reason, conf), apply_patch() resolves sec_id via exact or substring key matching against St-1, applies the atomic operation (op ∈ {update, append, delete, replace_all}), increments version vt = vt-1 + 1, and measures normalized edit churn Δdiff,t ∈ [0, 1] using difflib.SequenceMatcher:

Δdiff,t = clamp[0,1](1 − SequenceMatcher(Dt-1, Dt).ratio()) (2)

C. The 4-Tier Resilient Patching Engine

Small non-instruction-tuned SLMs frequently emit malformed JSON, wrap responses in Markdown fences, or embed unescaped newlines inside string fields. Rather than failing hard, ResilientPatcher.parse_patch() (codraft/patcher.py) executes a strict 4-tier progressive recovery cascade:

  • Tier 1 (Direct Strict JSON, conf=0.80): Attempts native json.loads() on stripped raw output and verifies presence of the required content key.
  • Tier 2 (Markdown Fenced-Block Extraction, conf=0.80): Extracts payloads enclosed within ```json ... ``` code fences via regex and parses the structured patch object.
  • Tier 3 (Curly-Brace Slicing & Newline Sanitization, conf=0.75/0.70): Locates outermost {...} delimiters to strip conversational preamble/postamble; if strict parsing fails, sanitizes unescaped line breaks via negative lookbehind regex (?<!\\)\n prior to deserialization.
  • Tier 4 (Heuristic Prose Fallback, conf=0.50): If all JSON recovery tiers fail, wraps cleaned natural-language output into a safe PatchOperation targeting section overview using replace_all (if length >100 characters) or append, guaranteeing 0% unrecoverable patch crashes while penalizing confidence to alert the controller.

D. Claim-Centric Evidence Ledger & Verification Pressure

After each committed patch, EvidenceLedger.extract_claims() (codraft/evidence.py) strips fenced code blocks, segments prose into sentences (≥ 5 tokens), and filters deduplicated factual assertions matching quantitative or relational markers (digits, is a, defined as, consists of, causes, results in). Each claim is tracked with its authoring model, version stamp, and verification status (unverified, uncertain, verified). High-confidence refining patches (conf > 0.85) promote pending claims to verified. The ledger exposes Laplace-smoothed verification pressure Vt ∈ [0, 1):

Vt = |{c ∈ Lt : status(c) ∈ {unverified, uncertain}}| / (|Lt| + 1) (3)

E. Multi-Signal Adaptive Controller & Stopping Gates

At the end of each collaborative turn, AdaptiveController (codraft/controller.py) quantifies epistemic uncertainty Ut by blending lexical hedging density across 11 uncertainty cues (maybe, perhaps, possibly, unclear, likely, probably, might, could be, it seems, approximate, uncertain) with the complement of patch confidence:

Ut = 0.5·min(1, 3Nhedge/max(1, Nwords)) + 0.5·(1 − conft) (4)

The controller aggregates all four state signals into a composite collaboration urgency score St ∈ [0, 1]:

St = 0.35·C(q) + 0.25·Ut + 0.25·Δdiff,t + 0.15·Vt (5)

Execution halts immediately when any of four deterministic stopping conditions triggers: (i) round budget exhausted (t ≥ Tmax); (ii) stagnation (Δdiff,t < 0.04 for p=2 consecutive rounds); (iii) verified stability (Vt = 0.0, t ≥ 2, Δdiff,t < 0.10); or (iv) adaptive convergence (St < 0.20, t ≥ 2). Algorithm 1 formalizes the complete execution loop implemented in CoDraftOrchestrator.execute().

Algorithm 1: CoDraft+ Adaptive Collaborative Orchestration
Input: Query q, Models (MA, MB), Max Rounds T, Weights (0.35, 0.25, 0.25, 0.15)
Output: OrchestrationResult(D*, L*, patch_history, profiler_metrics)
1: Start ResourceProfiler; C(q), Mcurr ← QueryAnalyzer.analyze(q)
2: D0 ← Storage.init_session(sid); L0 ← EvidenceLedger()
3: Teff ← 1 if C(q) < 0.40 else T  // Fast-path check
4: for t = 1 to Teff do
5:   raw ← Engine.chat(Mcurr, sys_schema, q, Dt-1)
6:   Profiler.record_tokens(in_tok, out_tok); update_peak_ram()
7:   Πt ← ResilientPatcher.parse_patch(raw)  // 4-Tier Cascade
8:   Dt, Δdiff,t ← ResilientPatcher.apply_patch(Dt-1, Πt)
9:   Storage.save_state(Dt, Πt)  // SQLite + JSONL
10:  Lt.extract_claims(Dt); verify_if(conf > 0.85)
11:  stop, St ← Controller.evaluate_stopping(t, C(q), Δdiff,t, Dt, Lt)
12:  if stop then break
13:  Mcurr ← MB if Mcurr == MA else MA
14: end for
15: return OrchestrationResult(Dt, Lt, history, Profiler.stop())

F. Context Complexity: Debate vs. Canonical Blackboard

In a K-agent conversational debate where each agent emits L tokens per round, round-t prompt input accumulates ∑i=1..t−1 KL = O(KLt) tokens, yielding total prompt processing cost ∑t=1..T O(KLt) = O(KLT2). In CoDraft+, each turn reads only the current canonical draft |Dt−1| ≈ L plus a constant schema prompt Lsys (∼85 tokens), bounding per-turn input at O(L + Lsys) and total T-round prompt processing strictly linearly at O(TL). This asymptotic reduction from quadratic to linear context growth directly enables multi-round refinement within edge RAM and context ceilings.

V. Experimental Design & Model Tracks

To prevent architectural cherry-picking and test generalization across distinct pre-training regimes, tokenizers, and parameter ratios, we evaluate CoDraft+ across three independent open-weight sub-50M model families frozen offline (Validation Gate G1):

  • Set 1 (Pythia / GPT-NeoX) [5]: EleutherAI/pythia-14m (14,067,712 params, Drafting) + EleutherAI/pythia-31m (30,488,064 params, Patching) = 44.56M combined vs. EleutherAI/pythia-70m (70,426,624 params, 1.58× combined scale). All trained on 300B deduplicated tokens of The Pile.
  • Set 2 (BabyLM / GPT-BERT) [6]: babylm-baseline-10m-gpt-bert-causal-focus (39,339,072 total / 32.88M backbone; 93.75% causal, 6.25% masked) + babylm-baseline-10m-gpt-bert-mixed (39,339,072 total; 50% causal, 50% masked) = 78.68M combined vs. babylm-baseline-100m-gpt-bert-causal-focus (132,040,704 total / 118.85M backbone, 1.81× scale). Trained on 10M-word BabyLM Strict-Small with a rigid 512-token context window.
  • Set 3 (Stentor2 / LlamaForCausalLM) [13]: StentorLabs/Stentor2-12M (12,288,000 params) + StentorLabs/Stentor2-30M (30,349,440 params) = 42.64M combined, using RoPE, GQA/MHA, FineWeb-Edu curated pre-training, and the native TokenMonster server runtime. Because StentorLabs did not release an ~85M checkpoint, E5 is strictly omitted per our Zero-Synthetic-Data Mandate.

Standardized E1–E5 Protocol: Under identical hardware and prompt conditions, every track evaluates: E1 (Small A Solo), E2 (Small B Solo), E3 (Isolated Parallel Ensemble A+B without cross-communication, isolating pure ensembling from interactive refinement), E4 (CoDraft+ Shared Canonical Workspace), and E5 (Monolithic Large Baseline Solo). Answer quality is evaluated via Token F1, ROUGE-L [10], and BLEU-1 [11]. Intrinsic capabilities are evaluated on held-out corpora (The Pile, BabyLM Dev, PG-19) via bits-per-byte (BPB) and perplexity (PPL), plus zero-shot log-likelihood accuracy on PIQA [7], ARC-Challenge [8], WinoGrande [9], CoLA, BoolQ, SciQ, and OpenBookQA with 95% Wald confidence intervals (Gate G8).

B. Set 2 (BabyLM / GPT-BERT): Context Horizon Collapse

In sharp contrast to Set 1, Table IV and Fig. 7 expose an instructive negative result on Set 2. Under BabyLM, E4 collapses completely to 0.00% F1 / ROUGE-L / BLEU-1 while latency rises to 1047.02 s across 2.7 rounds. Runtime diagnostics trace this collapse directly to two compounding constraints: (1) BabyLM checkpoints were pre-trained on a tiny 10M-word developmental corpus without instruction tuning, and (2) modeling_gpt_bert.py enforces a rigid 512-token context ceiling. Once the system JSON schema, user prompt, and prior draft exceed 512 tokens in multi-turn collaboration, positional representations truncate and the 39M models emit degenerate formatting loops. By contrast, single-turn generation on the 132.04M causal baseline (E5) stays inside 512 tokens and achieves 9.67% F1. This proves an empirical prerequisite: collaborative workspace patching requires models with context capacity ≥ 1024 tokens (or abstractive workspace compression).

C. Set 3 (Stentor2 / Llama): Asymmetric Capability Dilution

Set 3 (Table V, Fig. 8) reveals a second, equally fundamental multi-agent law. Thanks to modern Llama-style rotary embeddings and curated FineWeb-Edu pre-training, Stentor2 models execute remarkably fast (0.85–2.50 s) in under 843 MB peak RAM. However, Stentor2-30M vastly outperforms Stentor2-12M solo (13.30% F1, 9.98% ROUGE-L, 27.61% BLEU-1 vs. 5.04% F1, 4.61% ROUGE-L). When both models take unweighted alternating turns in E4, edits from the weaker 12M partner overwrite and degrade the stronger 30M draft, pulling ROUGE-L down to 5.17%. Thus, unweighted symmetric collaboration succeeds when models possess complementary competencies (Set 1), but degrades output when one partner strictly dominates the other across all dimensions.

VII. Edge Systems Mechanics & Guidelines

A. Interleaved Low-RAM Execution & Global Pareto Frontier

Because CoDraft+ invokes models sequentially across turns via engine.chat(..., keep_alive), peak system RAM is bounded by max(RSS(MA), RSS(MB)) + Overhead(Dt) rather than their sum. Workspace state serialized to SQLite and JSONL remains below 15 KB per session. Fig. 10 aggregates all 14 experimental configurations across Sets 1, 2, and 3 into a unified Pareto space: CoDraft+ Pythia (E4) achieves 8.25% ROUGE-L at 1234.1 MB RAM, while Stentor2 executes sub-second rounds under 843 MB RAM—feasible even on 2 GB single-board edge computers.

Fig. 10. Unified cross-track Pareto frontier (ROUGE-L vs. Peak RAM). Maps all Pythia, BabyLM, and Stentor2 configurations in one memory-quality plane, highlighting CoDraft+ Pythia's efficiency envelope.

B. Engineering Guidelines for Multi-SLM Edge Deployment

Synthesizing our positive and negative findings yields three concrete design laws for practitioners building multi-SLM systems on edge hardware:

  • Law 1 (Minimum Context Budget ≥ 1024 Tokens): Structured workspace patching adds ~180–300 tokens of schema and draft state per turn. Never pair models capped at 512 tokens without aggressive abstractive section compression.
  • Law 2 (Prefer Complementary Pairs over Dominated Pairs): Audit candidate SLMs on orthogonal zero-shot benchmarks (e.g., PIQA vs. ARC-C). Pair models with complementary strengths (as in Pythia 14M+31M) rather than pairing a strictly dominated model with a strong specialist (as in Stentor2 12M+30M).
  • Law 3 (Deploy Multi-Tier Parsing by Default): Sub-50M models routinely break strict JSON formatting. A progressive 4-tier fallback parser with confidence down-weighting converts brittle syntax errors into graceful, auditable state updates.

VIII. Threats to Validity & Limitations

Following empirical software engineering reporting standards, we explicitly evaluate threats to the validity of our study:

Internal Validity: Multi-turn latency measurements on shared CPU hardware can fluctuate due to OS background scheduling. To mitigate variance, all E1–E5 configurations ran sequentially under identical FP32 AVX2/oneDNN CPU settings with batch size 1 and automated RSS peak polling (ResourceProfiler). All 10 validation gates (G1–G10) and 12/12 unit tests verified deterministic patching and ledger accounting.

External Validity: Our benchmarks focus on sub-50M parameter checkpoints (12M–39M) to stress-test extreme edge hardware without GPU acceleration. While the Pythia results prove superadditive collaboration is achievable at tiny scale, larger 1B–7B quantized edge models (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B, Gemma3-1B via OllamaEngine) possess far stronger instruction-following abilities and are expected to exhibit higher Tier-1 JSON compliance and lower context-collapse risk.

Construct Validity: Lexical overlap metrics (Token F1, ROUGE-L, BLEU-1) capture structural fidelity against reference answers but may under-credit valid paraphrasing. We mitigated single-metric bias by pairing generative overlap with held-out language modeling perplexity (BPB/PPL) and zero-shot multiple-choice log-likelihood accuracy across 7 standard benchmarks.

Conclusion Validity: Several zero-shot categorical reasoning slices in Table VII exhibit wide 95% Wald confidence intervals (e.g., 66.67% [13.3, 100.0]) due to compact local evaluation sample budgets under CPU-only FP32 inference. Accordingly, we treat common-suite reasoning profiles as diagnostic indicators explaining complementarity rather than claiming statistically significant leaderboard supremacy. Finally, per our Zero-Synthetic-Data Certification, zero numbers were fabricated, interpolated, or simulated; the complete codebase, tests, and raw evaluation ledgers are openly inspectable at github.com/satyamsrj/codraft-plus.

IX. Conclusion & Future Work

We presented CoDraft+, an open-source, resource-aware collaborative framework enabling heterogeneous small language models to iteratively draft, patch, and verify one shared canonical answer on edge devices with <8 GB RAM. By replacing quadratic conversational debate with linear blackboard editing, a 4-tier ResilientPatcher, a claim-centric EvidenceLedger, and a 4-signal AdaptiveController, CoDraft+ unlocks superadditive small-model synergy: on Pythia, pairing 14M and 31M models achieves 8.25% ROUGE-L (+79.7% over the 1.58× monolithic 70M baseline) at a lower peak memory footprint (1234.1 MB vs. 1250.1 MB). Equally, documenting BabyLM context collapse and Stentor2 capability dilution provides actionable engineering guardrails for the edge-AI community.

Future work will introduce (1) capability-weighted asymmetric patch gating to eliminate dilution in unequal model pairs such as Stentor2, (2) section-level context compression to sustain collaboration on short-window models, and (3) large-scale evaluation of 4-bit quantized 1B–3B specialist pairs via OllamaEngine on embedded ARM and laptop hardware.
